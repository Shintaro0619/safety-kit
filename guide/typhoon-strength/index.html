What do 'strong', 'very strong' and 'violent' typhoon mean in Japan?

JMA grades a typhoon by its maximum sustained wind: strong (33 m/s or more, under 44), very strong (44 or more, under 54) and violent (54 m/s or more). On JMA's maps the storm area is where winds of 25 m/s or more are blowing or may blow, and the strong-wind area is 15 m/s or more. If your city is inside the storm circle on the forecast, plan to be indoors for that window; trains and flights stop before it arrives.

The two circles on the map

Storm area (bōfū-iki): winds of 25 m/s or more are blowing or may blow. Strong-wind area (kyōfū-iki): 15 m/s or more.

The one rule

Inside the storm circle, be indoors for that window. Japanese railways announce planned suspensions ahead of a typhoon and airlines cancel in the same window; check the evening before and book the extra night early.
